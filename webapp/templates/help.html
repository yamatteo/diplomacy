I termini di questo sito sono quelli del regolamento italiano: Regole del gioco (PDF, 11 pagine), con un esempio di partita commentato (“Esempio di gioco”, p. 10). Le regole del sito però sono quelle della 4ª edizione ufficiale (2000): Rules of Diplomacy (PDF in inglese, 24 pagine). Dove i due regolamenti non coincidono valgono le regole del sito, riassunte in questa pagina.

Differenze rispetto a questo regolamento:

  • Trasporto via mare lungo più rotte: se una delle flotte viene sloggiata ma resta almeno una rotta intatta, l'armata arriva lo stesso (il regolamento italiano, XII.4 ed Esempio 12, dice che il trasporto non avviene).
  • Partita a due giocatori: inizia nella Primavera 1901 (non nel 1914); l'Italia resta neutrale fino alla Primavera 1902, quando una moneta decide a quale dei due giocatori si unisce; vince chi ha 24 centri di approvvigionamento (non 24 unità).
  • Abbreviazioni: il sito usa le sue (vedi la tabella in fondo). Il regolamento scrive Mid per il nostro MAO, Nat per NAO, Nrg per NWG, Gul o Lyo per LYO, e Tyr sia per il Tirolo (il nostro TYR) sia per il Mar Tirreno (il nostro TYS).

Careful: the rulebook's abbreviations are not always ours (it writes Mid, NAt, Nrg, GoL, Tyn; our map says MAO, NAO, NWG, LYO, TYS). Use the table at the bottom.

{{ _("You can support, or convoy, another player's unit, without asking. A support cannot be refused.") }}

{{ _('Who wins a conflict') }}

  • {{ _('Strength is 1 plus the number of valid supports. The strongest wins; if the best are ' 'equal, nobody moves (a bounce). A bounce does not dislodge a unit already there.') }}
  • {{ _('To dislodge a unit you need more strength than it has holding there. A dislodged unit ' 'must retreat.') }}
  • {{ _('A unit that fails to move stays where it is, and can in turn block those trying to enter its ' 'province.') }}
  • {{ _('Two units cannot swap places (except by convoy). Three or more can rotate in a ' 'circle.') }}
  • {{ _('A support is cut if the supporting unit is attacked from anywhere except the province ' 'it is supporting an attack on, even if that attack fails. It is also cut if the supporting unit is ' 'dislodged.') }}
  • {{ _('A support must match the order exactly: supporting A MUN - SIL does nothing if Munich goes ' 'elsewhere or holds.') }}
  • {{ _('You cannot dislodge your own unit, nor support someone doing so, and attacking your own ' 'unit does not cut its support.') }}
  • {{ _('A convoy fails if one of its fleets is dislodged (attacking the fleet is not enough) ' 'and no other route is left. The army then stays where it was.') }}
  • {{ _('Only fleets in sea provinces convoy; fleets in coastal provinces cannot.') }}

{{ _('Map oddities') }}

  • {{ _('Spain, Bulgaria and St Petersburg have two coasts. A fleet there sits on one coast ' '(F SPA/NC, F STP/SC, F BUL/EC) and can only leave along it. It ' 'still occupies the whole province.') }}
  • {{ _('Kiel and Constantinople have a single coast: fleets pass through them (Kiel canal, the ' 'straits).') }}
  • {{ _('Armies and fleets can go between Sweden and Denmark. A fleet cannot go from the Baltic ' 'straight to the Skagerrak.') }}
  • {{ _('A fleet on a coast moves only to provinces adjacent along that coast: Rome to Tuscany or ' 'Naples, not to Venice.') }}
  • {{ _('Switzerland cannot be entered.') }}

{{ _('Retreats') }}

  • {{ _('A dislodged unit retreats to an adjacent province it could normally move to, but not to an occupied ' 'one, not to where its attacker came from, and not to one left empty by a bounce this turn.') }}
  • {{ _('With nowhere to go it is disbanded. You may also choose to disband.') }}
  • {{ _('Two units retreating to the same province are both disbanded.') }}

{{ _('Builds and disbands (Winter)') }}

  • {{ _('More centers than units: you may build, only in your own home centers (★ in ' 'the table below), if you still own them and they are empty. Armies anywhere, fleets on the coast. You ' 'may build fewer than allowed.') }}
  • {{ _('Fewer centers than units: you must disband the difference.') }}
  • {{ _('A center you walked through in Spring but left before the end of Fall is not yours.') }}
