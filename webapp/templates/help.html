Cheat sheet

The full rules are in the official rulebook: Rules of Diplomacy (PDF, 24 pages). Its last pages have a sample game (p. 19) and the "22 rules" summary (p. 23). This page is the short version, plus how this site works.

Careful: the rulebook's abbreviations are not always ours (it writes Mid, NAt, Nrg, GoL, Tyn; our map says MAO, NAO, NWG, LYO, TYS). Use the table at the bottom.

The game in brief

  • Goal: control 18 of the 34 supply centers (the dots on the map). Players can also agree to a draw between the survivors.
  • Units: armies (A) move on land, fleets (F) on sea and along coasts. All units have the same strength, and a province holds one unit at most.
  • Everybody moves at once. Orders are secret, then revealed and resolved together. Talk, promise and lie as you like: nothing said is binding.
  • You have as many units as supply centers. Centers change hands only at the end of Fall, by having a unit there.

A game year

Retreat and adjustment phases are skipped when nobody has anything to do in them.

Orders

OrderWrittenMeaning
HoldA PAR HStay. A unit without orders holds.
MoveA PAR - BURGo to an adjacent province. Moving into an occupied province is an attack.
Support a moveA GAS S A MAR - BURAdd 1 to the strength of that exact move. You must be able to move to the target province yourself (here Burgundy).
Support a unit in placeF ROM S F TYSAdd 1 to the defence of a unit that is not moving (holding, supporting or convoying).
ConvoyF NTH C A LON - NWYA fleet at sea carries an army between two coasts. The army is ordered A LON - NWY VIA. Several fleets can form a chain.
RetreatA BUR R GASRetreat phase only.
DisbandA BUR DRemove the unit (retreat and adjustment phases).
BuildF BRE BAdjustment phase only.

You can support, or convoy, another player's unit, without asking. A support cannot be refused.

Who wins a conflict

  • Strength is 1 plus the number of valid supports. The strongest wins; if the best are equal, nobody moves (a bounce). A bounce does not dislodge a unit already there.
  • To dislodge a unit you need more strength than it has holding there. A dislodged unit must retreat.
  • A unit that fails to move stays where it is, and can in turn block those trying to enter its province.
  • Two units cannot swap places (except by convoy). Three or more can rotate in a circle.
  • A support is cut if the supporting unit is attacked from anywhere except the province it is supporting an attack on, even if that attack fails. It is also cut if the supporting unit is dislodged.
  • A support must match the order exactly: supporting A MUN - SIL does nothing if Munich goes elsewhere or holds.
  • You cannot dislodge your own unit, nor support someone doing so, and attacking your own unit does not cut its support.
  • A convoy fails if one of its fleets is dislodged (attacking the fleet is not enough) and no other route is left. The army then stays where it was.
  • Only fleets in sea provinces convoy; fleets in coastal provinces cannot.

Map oddities

  • Spain, Bulgaria and St Petersburg have two coasts. A fleet there sits on one coast (F SPA/NC, F STP/SC, F BUL/EC) and can only leave along it. It still occupies the whole province.
  • Kiel and Constantinople have a single coast: fleets pass through them (Kiel canal, the straits).
  • Armies and fleets can go between Sweden and Denmark. A fleet cannot go from the Baltic straight to the Skagerrak.
  • A fleet on a coast moves only to provinces adjacent along that coast: Rome to Tuscany or Naples, not to Venice.
  • Switzerland cannot be entered.

Retreats

  • A dislodged unit retreats to an adjacent province it could normally move to, but not to an occupied one, not to where its attacker came from, and not to one left empty by a bounce this turn.
  • With nowhere to go it is disbanded. You may also choose to disband.
  • Two units retreating to the same province are both disbanded.

Builds and disbands (Winter)

  • More centers than units: you may build, only in your own home centers (★ in the table below), if you still own them and they are empty. Armies anywhere, fleets on the coast. You may build fewer than allowed.
  • Fewer centers than units: you must disband the difference.
  • A center you walked through in Spring but left before the end of Fall is not yours.

Using this site

  • Game page. The map, the deadline, your orders and a table of all powers. Negotiate however you like (in person, chat, phone): the site only collects orders.
  • Giving orders. Each of your units has a menu listing every legal order for it. Leaving the first entry means the default: hold in movement, disband in retreats, no build or keep in winter. Press Save orders. Your saved orders appear as arrows on your own map.
  • Secrecy. Nobody else sees your orders until the phase is processed. Others only see whether you have submitted. You can change your orders as often as you like until then.
  • "I'm done". With the box ticked, you are ready: as soon as every player is ready the phase is processed, without waiting for the deadline. Untick it to keep your orders saved but make the game wait for you.
  • Deadline. When it passes, the phase is processed with whatever was saved, the next time anyone opens a page. Standard lengths are {{ phase_hours['M'] }} hours for movement and {{ phase_hours['R'] }} for retreats and adjustments; the admin can change them.
  • If you miss it: your units hold; dislodged units are disbanded; builds are lost; if you had to disband, the engine chooses which units.
  • Powers without a player never give orders: their units hold, and nobody waits for them. With fewer than seven players the admin starts the game for 3-7 (or 2) players as in the rulebook's "Alternate way to play": neutral powers hold and never support, and one user may control several countries, all in the same orders form.
  • History shows every past phase: the map with everybody's orders, and the results.
